Training your adolescent Doberman

When familiar skills become harder, make practice easier and look at the setting, your dog’s needs and their comfort. Adolescence is a reason to adapt support, not to assume defiance.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 12, 2026

Describe the change before explaining it

Write down what your dog does, where it happens and what changed in the routine. Responding in the kitchen but struggling near dogs is different from struggling everywhere. Avoid calling your dog stubborn or dominant: those labels do not identify a useful next training step.

Return to an easy success

Practice a familiar skill in a quiet place with a reward your dog wants. Shorten the task or increase distance from a distraction. Ask once rather than repeating a cue through escalating excitement. End while your dog remains comfortable and can disengage.

Change one challenge at a time

Keep duration, distance and distraction separate. If you move from the garden to a busy pavement, reduce the expectation instead of demanding the same performance. Use appropriate equipment and managed access while reliability develops; an earlier success does not establish off-lead safety.

A worked practice example

Imagine recall works in an empty garden but fails near playing dogs. Start in a quieter secured area, reward a comfortable turn toward you and record the distance from activity. Progress only when the current step is easy. A missed response means redesigning practice, not punishing the return.

Make rest and support part of the plan

Review sleep opportunities, toileting, enrichment and predictable routines rather than adding ever more intense exercise. Pause if frustration or tension increases. New discomfort, sudden change or difficulty across familiar settings warrants a veterinary conversation. A qualified reward-based trainer can help tailor practice.

Common questions

Has my dog forgotten everything?

A difficult response in one setting does not prove that. Compare easier familiar settings and adjust the task; seek advice for sudden or widespread changes.
