Polite greetings without jumping

Plan greetings around space, choice and four paws on the floor. A visitor does not need to touch your Doberman, and your dog does not need to greet everyone.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 12, 2026

Plan the doorway before opening it

Arrange a comfortable separate space or a suitable barrier before a visitor arrives. Ask the visitor to wait while you settle the entrance routine. Keep children out of training demonstrations and use adult supervision and separation for safety. A crowded doorway is a difficult place to learn.

Practice with a willing adult

Choose one calm adult who agrees to your plan. Start far enough apart that your dog can observe without lunging or jumping. Reward four paws on the ground and comfortable attention. Let the person approach only while your dog remains comfortable; a greeting is optional, not the reward you must force.

Pause the approach when excitement rises

If paws lift or the lead tightens, ask the person to stop approaching and create more space. Reward an easier moment of calm before trying again. Avoid kneeing, shouting or jerking the lead. If contact keeps triggering jumping, practice simply watching the person at a greater distance.

Use this visitor rehearsal

Example: a friend stands across the room while you reward your dog’s paws staying down. The friend takes one step, pauses, then steps away if your dog becomes excited. Repeat only while both are comfortable. Record distance, body posture and whether approach remained easy rather than counting touches.

Respect a decision not to greet

Turning away, backing up or a tense posture can mean your dog needs space. End the interaction and offer quiet rest; do not hold your dog still for petting. For repeated distress, growling or safety concerns, use separation and seek qualified support. Sudden changes also warrant a veterinary conversation.

Common questions

Must my dog sit for every greeting?

No. Comfortable four-paw standing can be a useful alternative. Choose a behavior your dog can offer easily in that setting.

What if a visitor encourages jumping?

Explain the plan before contact. If they cannot follow it, use a comfortable separate space rather than rehearsing more jumping.
