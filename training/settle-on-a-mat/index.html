Teach your Doberman to settle on a mat

Build a comfortable resting place through small, rewarded choices. A relaxed dog matters more than a long down-stay: start quietly, allow breaks and increase difficulty gradually.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 12, 2026

Start with comfort and choice

Choose a nonslip mat large enough for your dog to rest comfortably. Put it in a quiet room after toileting and with water available. Allow your dog to approach voluntarily; reward interest and stepping onto it. Do not pull your dog onto the mat or use it as punishment.

Reward resting, not constant excitement

Place a small reward calmly on the mat for comfortable standing, sitting or lying down. Watch for a softer posture and the ability to look away from you. If every reward makes your dog leap up, make delivery calmer and the session easier. A rigid dog staring at food is not necessarily relaxed.

Add a cue after the action is familiar

Once approaching the mat is predictable, introduce a consistent cue just before that familiar action. Use a separate release word to end practice, then offer a break. Build tiny comfortable pauses before increasing duration; do not add a busy doorway and a longer wait at the same time.

Try this practice scenario

Imagine your dog readily lies down in the kitchen but stands when you lift a cup. Practice a smaller movement, such as touching the cup, and reward comfortable resting. If your dog leaves, reset without correction. Change only one feature: movement, duration or location.

Know when to pause

Leave the mat available for ordinary rest outside lessons. Stop if your dog repeatedly gets up, pants unexpectedly, avoids the mat or cannot disengage from food. Check comfort and needs, return to an easier setting and seek veterinary advice for new discomfort. A reward-based trainer can help with persistent difficulty.

Common questions

Is staying down the same as relaxing?

No. Look for comfortable posture and the ability to disengage, rather than judging relaxation only by time spent lying down.

Can I start in a café?

Begin somewhere quiet and familiar. Introduce new places gradually and leave if your dog cannot remain comfortable.
