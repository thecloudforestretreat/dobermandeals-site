Body condition: look beyond the number on the scales

Body condition assesses fat stores; weight alone does not show it. Ask your veterinary team to demonstrate the WSAVA chart on your own dog.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 12, 2026

Use the chart with a demonstration

The WSAVA dog chart uses a nine-point scale and combines observation with gentle assessment by touch. Ask the clinic to show you how it applies to your dog. Do not assign a score from a portrait photograph or press on a dog who is uncomfortable with handling.

Look at ribs, waist and abdominal outline

The chart considers rib coverage, the waist seen from above and the abdominal outline from the side. These observations work together. Coat, posture and the viewing angle can affect what you see, which is why a photograph or a single feature is insufficient on its own.

Distinguish fat from muscle assessment

Body condition and muscle condition address different information. Ask the clinician to record both when appropriate and explain changes. Do not assume a dog’s muscle status from a body-condition number or classify every change in weight as a simple feeding problem.

Keep a trend and discuss unexplained changes

Record date, weight, the clinician’s score and the next review agreed with the clinic. Use a consistent record instead of judging one day in isolation. Unexplained changes deserve a veterinary conversation; the record is not a self-prescribed weight-loss or weight-gain program.

Example: add context to a weigh-in

An illustrative owner brings a recent weight and asks for a demonstrated condition assessment. The clinic’s explanation and follow-up go beside the date in the owner’s notes. No fictional condition score is assigned here, and the example does not dictate a feeding adjustment.
