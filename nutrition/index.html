Write down the current diet

Include the exact food name, portion units, treats, chews and extras from everyone in the household. A diet record is more useful than “one bowl” when bowl size and additional food vary. Keep packaging information so the clinic can identify the product.

Use the feeding guide as a starting route

The feeding guide helps you organize label information and portion records. It does not prescribe one amount for all Dobermans. Growth, activity, condition and medical needs can affect the plan; discuss uncertainty before making major changes.

Assess condition with professional guidance

Weight and body condition describe different information. Use the condition guide and the WSAVA chart with a veterinary demonstration. A number on the scales or a photograph alone does not establish the right feeding adjustment for an individual dog.

Separate ordinary feeding from clinical diets

If a veterinarian has prescribed a diet, clarify the instructions and what additional foods are permitted. Do not replace a clinical plan with a breed marketing claim or add supplements based on an online list. Bring feeding concerns to the professional responsible for the plan.

Example: household treats

An illustrative family discovers that three people offer treats without recording them. They agree a shared daily log and take the full record to the clinic before changing portions. The example improves information; it does not estimate the dog’s calorie needs.
