Choose a useful route

For adoption, begin with our rescue-adoption guide and check an organization’s own contact information before applying. For training support, use the trainer-selection guide to prepare questions about methods and experience. For website questions or resource suggestions, use our contact form. The routes below explain what each guide helps you do.

Check a resource before relying on it

A directory entry should identify the organization, its official website, the area it serves and when those details were checked. Credentials need a verifiable source; inclusion alone does not establish quality or availability. Check current services directly with the provider. We are not presenting a new vetted-provider directory on this page.

Keep experience and professional advice distinct

An owner’s story can describe what happened with one dog. It cannot establish a diagnosis, a training outcome for another dog or a product’s general safety. Stories and photographs need the contributor’s permission before publication. A professional claim needs an identifiable source and relevant expertise.

Participate with care

Send a public resource URL and explain who it helps when suggesting a resource. Keep addresses, private messages and other people’s personal information out of submissions. If a link breaks or information changes, report the page and the issue through our contact form. For urgent care, contact a veterinarian rather than waiting for a community reply.

Common questions

Can I submit a useful resource?

Yes. Send its public website address through our contact form and explain the audience and area it serves. A suggestion is not a guarantee of inclusion.

Does an owner story count as expert advice?

No. Personal experience needs to stay clearly identified, with professional claims supported separately.
