Start with the task your dog needs

A coat check, a brief brush and a bath are different tasks. Choose the useful task today rather than completing a long list because a calendar says so. Gather equipment first, choose a stable surface and provide a way for your dog to take a break.

Make handling part of the routine

Introduce the sight and sound of tools before using them. Practice a comfortable touch without restraining your dog into a longer session. A quiet dog can still be uncomfortable; observe movement, posture and whether your dog chooses to stay nearby.

Separate routine care from a concern

Short coats still need observation. Note a new area of irritation, broken skin or an unexpected change and discuss it with a veterinarian. Grooming can help you notice a problem, but photographs and a brush cannot diagnose its cause.

Use the connected care guides

Start with brushing if you are building a simple routine. Read the bathing guide before choosing products and preparing the space. Nail care is a separate handling skill; seek a professional demonstration if you are unsure about the tool or the safe amount to trim.
