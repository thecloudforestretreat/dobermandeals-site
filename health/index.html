Recognize an urgent situation

If your dog collapses or has difficulty breathing, seek emergency veterinary care immediately. Contact the clinic while arranging safe transport when possible. Do not use a screening guide to decide that urgent signs can wait for a routine appointment.

Separate prevention and screening

A preventive-care plan addresses the individual dog’s ongoing needs and exposures. Screening looks for particular information, sometimes before outward signs appear. Diagnosis evaluates a suspected condition. Ask the clinic which question an assessment is intended to answer.

Keep a records folder

Collect identification, dated reports, current medications and instructions from the clinic. Keep originals where possible and record what is missing. A consistent folder lets different caregivers find the same information without relying on remembered summaries.

Prepare your next appointment

Write the concern, when you noticed it and what has changed from the usual routine. Bring previous results and ask what follow-up is appropriate. The DCM and cardiac-screening guides explain questions to take to your veterinarian or cardiologist rather than supplying a treatment plan.

Example: two different appointment needs

One owner is arranging routine prevention; another has a dog that has just collapsed. The first can prepare a care checklist, while the second needs urgent veterinary help. This illustrative comparison explains why the timing and purpose of care matter; it is not a triage tool for every symptom.
