Suspected bloat or GDV: seek emergency veterinary care

If you suspect bloat or gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV), contact an emergency veterinarian and arrange immediate assessment. Do not wait for every sign or an online diagnosis.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 12, 2026

Recognize concerning signs without self-diagnosing

Repeated attempts to vomit without bringing anything up, a swelling abdomen, restlessness or marked distress can be concerning. Weakness or collapse is also urgent. Signs can vary and are not a home diagnostic test; uncertainty is a reason to contact a veterinarian, not to wait for a complete checklist.

Contact the emergency clinic and go

Tell the clinic what you observed and when it started while arranging transport. Confirm the destination and follow the team’s instructions. If another person is available, ask them to handle the call or records so help is not delayed by searching for information.

Leave treatment to the veterinary team

Do not attempt stomach decompression, force vomiting or give a home remedy. Ask the emergency team what to do during transport. This page provides an action route to professional care, not a technique for treating suspected GDV at home.

Prepare the contact plan before an emergency

At a routine time, save your clinic and appropriate emergency facility’s contact details and confirm their location and arrangements. Share them with caregivers. Keep identification and medication details accessible; the preparation checklist below is for planning ahead, not delaying urgent care.

Discuss future risk after urgent concerns are addressed

Ask your veterinarian about individual risk and whether a preventive gastropexy conversation is appropriate. That is a separate planning discussion, not a response to current warning signs. Do not replace assessment of a suspected emergency with feeding changes or future prevention research.
