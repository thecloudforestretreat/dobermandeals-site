Von Willebrand disease: prepare the right veterinary questions

Von Willebrand disease is an inherited bleeding disorder that can affect Dobermans. Bring test reports and a bleeding history to your veterinarian; a genetic result alone does not establish an individual procedure plan.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 13, 2026

When bleeding needs immediate help

Contact an emergency veterinary service for heavy or persistent bleeding, collapse or a rapidly worsening condition. Do not wait to complete this guide or try to provoke bleeding to test clotting. Tell the team about known test results and current medication.

Understand what each report says

Cornell describes von Willebrand factor as a protein involved in clotting. Genetic screening and measurement of the factor in blood provide different information. Ask your veterinarian what was tested, what the result means for this dog and whether further assessment is needed. A dog’s lack of obvious previous bleeding does not settle future procedure risk.

Discuss a procedure before the day

Tell the veterinary team about prior unusual bleeding and share the original reports before surgery or dental procedures. Ask which precautions, additional testing or referral they recommend. Bring a current medication and supplement list. Let the team decide treatment and medication changes; do not start, stop or substitute products from an online guide.

Make your history easy to use

Record when an episode occurred, its circumstances, how long it lasted and whether veterinary care was needed. Distinguish things you observed from things another caregiver reported. The illustrative record below shows how to organize those facts; it is not a diagnostic score.

Common questions

Does a genetic result replace a clinical assessment?

No. Ask your veterinarian to interpret the report alongside the history and any relevant blood tests.

Should I make a small cut to check clotting?

No. Testing belongs with the veterinary team; do not provoke bleeding at home.
