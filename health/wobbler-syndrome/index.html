Wobbler syndrome: report movement changes clearly

Wobbler syndrome involves the spinal cord in the neck region. New unsteadiness, weakness or neck discomfort needs veterinary assessment; an unusual walk alone cannot identify its cause.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 13, 2026

Notice changes without testing the neck

Describe stumbling, scuffing, difficulty rising or changes in walking that you actually observe. Note onset and progression. Do not force neck movements, manipulate the spine or repeatedly exercise the dog to reproduce a problem. Sudden inability to stand, severe pain or rapid deterioration needs urgent veterinary attention.

Understand the assessment pathway

Ohio State describes neurological examination and imaging as parts of evaluation. Merck notes that plain radiographs do not confirm this diagnosis. Ask the veterinarian which findings support a neck or neurological concern, what other causes need consideration and whether specialist assessment or advanced imaging is appropriate.

Ask for an individual activity plan

Treatment and activity instructions depend on the diagnosis and the individual dog. Ask how to manage movement, transport and daily care while assessment is arranged. Do not replace that advice with a home exercise program or spinal manipulation. Keep any prescribed instructions available to every caregiver.

Bring an observation record

Record the first change, whether it is worsening and which everyday tasks have become difficult. An existing video may help explain what you saw if your veterinarian requests it; do not delay care or provoke movement to create one. The example below is an observation format, not a confirmed case.

Common questions

Can a video confirm Wobbler syndrome?

No. It may help describe movement, but veterinary assessment and appropriate testing are needed.

Should I try neck stretches at home?

Do not manipulate the neck or start an unsupervised treatment routine. Ask the veterinary team for individual instructions.
