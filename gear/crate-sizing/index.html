Crate sizing: measure your Doberman, then check usable space

Choose a crate by your dog’s comfortable standing, turning and resting space—not a breed-size label. Fit and learning to feel comfortable in a crate are separate questions.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 12, 2026

Measure without forcing a position

Observe your dog standing naturally and resting comfortably on a nonslip surface. Record their height and the length of their resting footprint without pulling legs into place. If measuring causes concern, pause and seek help. These measurements begin the comparison; they do not prove a crate is suitable.

Compare internal dimensions

Ask the manufacturer for usable internal length, width and height, accounting for bedding, door openings and any divider. External dimensions and a nominal size can overstate room available to the dog. Check assembly instructions, latch security and appropriate use before bringing the crate home.

Check movement with choice

A suitable setup should allow comfortable standing, turning and resting. Introduce access gradually, keep the door secured open initially and observe willingness to enter and leave. Do not close a reluctant dog inside to test fit. A correct measurement does not treat separation distress or establish crate tolerance.

An illustrative measurement comparison

Planning example only: a resting footprint recorded as 90 × 55 cm cannot fit within a usable base of 85 × 50 cm. A larger base still needs checks for height, turning, bedding and door access. These numbers are hypothetical, not a measured Doberman, a product test or a recommended size.

Reassess the setup as needs change

Growing dogs and changes in bedding or mobility can alter usable space. Review comfort and fit instead of assuming a purchase remains suitable indefinitely. Seek professional advice for distress or physical discomfort; a crate should not be used to force a dog through panic.

Common questions

Does every Doberman need the same crate size?

No. Measure the individual dog and inspect usable dimensions and comfortable movement.

Does a larger crate fix distress?

No. Space and comfort with confinement are separate issues; distress needs appropriate support.
