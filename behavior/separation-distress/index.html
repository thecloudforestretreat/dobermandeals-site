Separation distress: start with support and observation

Difficulty during absence deserves careful assessment. Reduce distress where possible and contact your veterinarian; do not use forced long absences as a test or treatment.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 12, 2026

Describe what happens during absence

Note the departure setup, when signs appear and what you can observe safely. Vocalization, movement or damage can have different explanations. Record what you know without assigning a diagnosis from one sign or making the dog repeat a distressing episode to obtain evidence.

Discuss the concern with your veterinarian

A veterinary conversation can address health and guide appropriate behavior support. Bring the timeline, current routine and changes you noticed. Ask who should help you develop an individualized plan, how progress will be assessed and when to contact them again.

Arrange practical care cover

Where feasible, adjust arrangements to reduce situations in which the dog is distressed while you seek help. Discuss suitable caregivers and environments rather than assuming a crate or another dog will solve the problem. Keep the dog’s comfort and safe handling central to the arrangement.

Avoid pushing through distress

Do not punish damage or leave the dog to become increasingly distressed in the hope that it will learn. A professional plan may use carefully graded work suited to the individual dog. There is no fixed absence schedule here and no promise that a particular number of days will resolve the concern.

Example: a short departure log

An illustrative owner records the normal departure time, the first observed sign and the arrangements used that day. They share the log with the veterinarian and organize care cover. The record supports discussion; it is not an instruction to keep leaving until the dog stops responding.
