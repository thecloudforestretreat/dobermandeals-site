Reactivity: create space before practicing

Notice the situations that prompt a strong response, use safe management and seek qualified help when control or bite risk is a concern.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 12, 2026

Describe the response and setting

Record what your dog noticed, the approximate distance and the observable response. Barking or lunging describes an event; it does not identify motivation by itself. Include barriers, traffic and available space so a professional can understand the setup.

Choose a safer route or distance

Avoid trapping your dog close to a trigger. Look ahead and choose space where you can turn away safely. Use suitable, well-fitted equipment and a manageable route. Do not arrange a close encounter simply to find the dog’s limit.

Practice within an individual plan

A qualified reward-based professional can help set a suitable starting point and progression. Keep practice manageable and change the setup if your dog cannot participate comfortably. Distance is one observation, not a universal number that every dog must tolerate.

Treat safety concerns seriously

Maintain management and ask for individualized help if there is threatening behavior, a bite history or difficulty holding the dog safely. Sudden changes also warrant a veterinary conversation. A training article cannot certify that greetings with unfamiliar people or dogs are safe.

Example: turning before a narrow passage

An illustrative owner sees another dog approaching a narrow entrance. They choose a safe alternative route instead of asking for a close pass, then record the context for their trainer. The example shows a management choice; it does not prescribe an exposure exercise.
