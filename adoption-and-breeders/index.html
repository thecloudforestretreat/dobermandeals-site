Start with your household, not a listing

Describe the care you can offer, experience you have and limitations you need to discuss. A photograph may start a conversation, but it cannot show the dog’s full history or how a placement will work. Keep your questions consistent when comparing different options.

Understand what each route can tell you

A rescue may have observations from a foster home and available medical records; ask what remains unknown. A breeder should explain the parents, puppy raising and relevant records. Neither route removes uncertainty. Compare evidence and support rather than treating a label as a guarantee.

Verify the people and the paperwork

Find contact information through an independent official source, then compare it with the person contacting you. Ask to see records linked to an identifiable dog, written placement terms and the process for questions afterward. Avoid rushing into payment because someone claims another buyer is waiting.

Pause when a payment request feels wrong

Unexpected shipping charges, pressure and payment methods that are difficult to reverse deserve a pause. Verify the organization using contact details you found independently. For suspected scams in the United States, use the FTC’s official reporting route; elsewhere, contact the relevant local consumer authority.

An example: compare two conversations

Make two columns headed “confirmed” and “still to ask.” Record what each contact provided, how you verified it and the next question. This original comparison method helps you organize evidence. It is not an endorsement of a rescue, breeder or individual dog.
