Choose boarding or a sitter with a clear care plan

Compare how each provider will handle your dog’s routine, comfort and emergencies. A recommendation is a starting point; check the actual service, premises and written arrangements before booking.

By Doberman Deals · Updated October 13, 2026

Ask how care works each day

Discuss who supervises, time spent alone, rest space, exercise and contact with other dogs. Ask how staff respond to fear, barking or handling difficulty, and whether their methods match your care preferences. Explain your dog’s needs honestly so the provider can assess whether the setting is suitable.

Verify the provider locally

Ask for relevant licensing and insurance information, then check it with the appropriate authority or issuer. Requirements depend on location and service type. For example, the linked GOV.UK guidance covers commercial boarding licensing in England; it does not establish the rules for your own city or country.

Agree on emergency arrangements

Provide veterinary contact details and an alternative contact who can be reached during your absence. Agree how urgent care, transport, consent and costs will be handled. Ask what happens if the main caregiver becomes unavailable. Keep instructions for prescribed care exact and written, with the veterinary team’s guidance.

Prepare the handover

Record food, quantities, routine, handling preferences and important restrictions. Confirm pickup, updates and what supplies the provider accepts. If a gradual introduction is appropriate, agree it in advance. The example below is a planning scenario, not a provider recommendation or a promise that boarding will suit every dog.

No. Check the actual service and how it meets your dog’s needs.

No. Verify the requirements for the service’s location.
